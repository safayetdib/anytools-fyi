faq:
  - question: "Does this upload my PDF?"
    answer: "No. Your PDF is read and compressed inside your browser, and the smaller file is saved straight to your device. You can check this in your browser's Network tab: no file is uploaded."
  - question: "How much smaller will my PDF get?"
    answer: "It depends on what's inside. Scans and PDFs full of photos often shrink by half or much more. PDFs that are mostly text are usually small already, so they shrink less."
  - question: "Which compression level should I choose?"
    answer: "Recommended suits most files: photos stay sharp on screen and when printed at home. Extreme makes the smallest file, for email and upload limits. Less keeps the most detail, for printing photos."
  - question: "Will the text lose quality?"

  - question: "Why didn't my PDF get smaller?"

  - question: "Can I compress several PDFs at once?"
    answer: "Yes. Add as many as you like. Each one is compressed on its own, and they're saved together in one ZIP."
---

Compressing a PDF makes the file smaller, so it fits email attachments and upload forms. The photos and scans inside are where most of the size is, so they're saved at a sensible resolution, while text stays sharp. Choose how much to compress, see the size before and after, and download the smaller PDF. Nothing leaves your device.
